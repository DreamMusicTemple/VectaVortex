Audio diagnostics — no sound? check here first

Tap UNLOCK AUDIO, then PLAY TEST TONE.
If the Scope shows movement when you play but you hear nothing: the synth's audio engine is running correctly — this is almost always the surrounding app's WebView not routing web audio to the speakers, not a bug in the synth itself. Try UNLOCK AUDIO and PLAY TEST TONE above; if the test tone is also silent, that confirms it's the host app's WebView, and switching to a different HTML preview app or a real browser (Safari) should work. This does not affect a native AUv3/Swift port, which talks to CoreAudio directly and has no WebView involved.

Instruction manual

Layers — Wavetable, FM, and Sampler run simultaneously as three independent layers, each with its own filter, envelopes, and 4-band EQ, so they can be blended together in one patch or used one at a time.
Wavetable — 2 oscillators, each scanning through a table of waveform "frames" (Position moves through them) to morph the timbre continuously, from smooth analog-style waves to complex digital textures.
FM — 2 engines (A and B), each with 4 operators, using frequency modulation (one operator's output modulates another's pitch) to create harmonically rich, often bell-like or metallic tones that are hard to get from subtractive synthesis alone.
Sampler — 16 slots for your own recorded or imported audio, each independently pitched, filtered, and shaped with its own envelope, rather than one sample stretched across the whole keyboard.
SEG (Wavetable) — next to Analog Drift, on both Wavetable oscillators. Shrinks how much of the table the Position dial can actually reach: at 100% (default) Position sweeps the entire table as normal; turning SEG down concentrates that sweep into a smaller starting portion of the table — at 5%, Position only ever scans the first 5% of it. Useful for locking onto a narrower, more repetitive-sounding segment of a table instead of its full morph range.
Keyboard zones — every single oscillator (2 on Wavetable, all 8 operators across FM's two engines, all 16 on Sampler) has its own independent Range Low/High, Hold/latch, Glide, and Key track settings. Tap the ZONES button just above the keyboard to open one scrollable screen listing all of them, with a colored range bar per oscillator and LEARN LO/LEARN HI buttons that capture the next note you play (from the on-screen keyboard or a connected MIDI controller). This lets you build splits and layers down to the individual oscillator, not just per layer.
Hold — each oscillator's own Hold checkbox (in Keyboard Zones) latches its notes on until the same key is pressed again. The HOLD·WT/FM/SMP buttons in the transport bar are a shortcut that toggles every oscillator in that layer at once.
MPE — next to Scope. Turns on full MIDI Polyphonic Expression from a connected MPE controller (Roli Seaboard, LinnStrument, Osmose, and similar). With MPE on, each note you play arrives on its own MIDI channel, and that channel's pitch bend, Y-axis/slide (CC74), and pressure (aftertouch) are tracked independently per note instead of as one shared global value — so bending, sliding or pressing on one held note never affects any other note you're holding at the same time. Per-note pitch bend applies directly and immediately; the per-note Y-axis and pressure show up in the Matrix as two new sources, MPE Timbre (CC74) and MPE Pressure, so you can route either one to any destination — filter cutoff, wavetable position, level, and so on — exactly like any other modulation source, but scoped to that one note. Turning MPE off returns pitch bend to the single global Pitch wheel and stops tracking per-note expression. With MPE off, or on a controller that doesn't send per-note MIDI channels, everything behaves as a normal, non-MPE synth.
EQ & Amp Envelope — the 4-band EQ and the shared Amp Envelope sit just above the ZONES button, and update to whichever layer (Wavetable/FM/Sampler) is currently selected.
Filter envelope shape — the panel beside the filter shows the live filter-envelope curve at full size; tap DRAW CUSTOM SHAPE to hand-draw your own attack/decay/sustain/release contour instead of the standard ADSR.
Low-Pass Gate (Vactrol) — an extra filter type on the Wavetable and FM layers, alongside the usual filter types. Striking a note pings the amp and filter together with a natural leaky decay — like a plucked string or struck woodblock — rather than the usual separate amp and filter envelopes. Resonance and key track don't apply in this mode; the Cutoff dial instead sets how bright the note is at the moment it's struck, and the LPG Decay dial sets how long that natural decay takes. Releasing the key doesn't cut the sound short — it keeps ringing out on its own, so it's built for percussive, plucked, or struck sounds rather than sustained pads.
Wavefolder — an FX chain effect (FX tab) inspired by West Coast synthesis. Instead of cutting frequencies like a filter, it pushes the signal past its normal amplitude limits and folds the excess back on itself, for bright, complex, metallic harmonics. Drive controls how hard the signal is pushed before folding, Symmetry offsets the signal for more lopsided, complex folding, and Mix blends it with the dry signal — same per-layer sends as every other effect in the chain.
Invert Keys — next to ZONES. Swaps the colors of the white and black keys (the shape and layout of the keyboard stay exactly the same — only which color you see changes).
Scale & Scale Lock — pick a Scale and Root above the keyboard to highlight which keys belong to it with a small green dot. Turn on Scale Lock alongside it and the keyboard goes further: every key outside the chosen scale turns dark and stops responding to touch entirely, while the keys inside the scale light up — white-key scale notes in light lime green, black-key scale notes in dark green — so only notes that belong to the scale can be played at all. Useful for staying in-key without having to think about it. Turning Scale Lock off (or Scale back to Off) returns every key to normal.
Sequencer patterns — 4 pattern slots per layer, up to 32 steps each.
Pattern chain — below the pattern bank on the Sequencer tab, per layer. Tap PATTERN 1-4 to build a play order (any pattern can repeat, in any sequence — e.g. 1→3→2→1). Chain mode decides what happens once that order finishes one full pass: Off ignores the chain entirely and just loops whichever single pattern is currently selected, exactly as before; "loop chain" repeats the whole defined order indefinitely; "one shot" plays through the order once and then holds silently on its last pattern rather than restarting. CLEAR CHAIN empties the order and returns to Off. Pressing PLAY always restarts a chain from its first entry.
Top preset bar — the row below the logo mirrors the Presets tab's navigation (◀/▶ steps through presets in the current folder, the name shows what's loaded) plus QUICK SAVE (overwrites the currently loaded preset — use SAVE AS on the Presets tab for a new name), LOAD (jumps to the Presets tab's full browser), and INIT (resets everything to a blank factory-default patch — asks for confirmation first, since anything unsaved is lost).
Undo / Redo — next to the top preset bar. Tracks the last 10 changes made to any dial, checkbox, or dropdown anywhere in the app (a continuous drag on one dial counts as a single step, not one per pixel moved). UNDO/REDO step backward and forward through that history; both grey out automatically when there's nothing further to step to. Loaded sample audio itself isn't part of this history, since dials/checkboxes/dropdowns never change which samples are loaded.
Randomise (Sequencer) — reshuffles every step's on/off, ratchet count, probability, and any P-locks that step already had (existing ones are nudged, new ones are never invented). Pitches are drawn from whichever Scale is currently chosen above the keyboard, so a randomised pattern still stays musically in-key; with no scale selected, pitches spread chromatically instead. On the Sampler layer specifically, Randomise picks a random transient slice per step instead of a pitch, since Sampler steps trigger slices of the loaded sample rather than pitched notes.
Ratchet — per step, choose ×1/×2/×3/×4 as four buttons (only one active at a time). A ratchet re-triggers that step's note multiple times within its own step length — ×4 fires the note four times in the space of one step, for rolls and stutters.
Tempo multiplier — also in the step-edit panel (double-tap a step). Scales that individual step's duration relative to the pattern's base speed, so a single step can run at double or half time while the rest of the pattern keeps its normal grid.
Sampler transient slicing — on the Sampler layer's sequencer page, a row of selectors sits directly above the step grid, one per step, for all 32 steps. Each lets you assign that step to trigger a specific detected transient (slice) from the sample loaded in oscillator 1, instead of the sample's normal start point — great for chopping a break into individual hits across a pattern. Detect up to 32 transients per sample (DETECT TRANSIENTS button, or the full-screen sample editor) — enough to give a 32-step pattern a unique slice on every single step if you want. Drag any detected transient's marker to fine-tune its position; each one can also be set to reverse and independently panned in the full-screen editor.
Matrix — wire any source (LFOs, envelopes, velocity, mod wheel, macros, the global envelope follower) to any destination. Use the Overview grid view to see all connections at a glance, or the Wire List for an AUM-style view with a colored cable per connection.
LFO Bipolar — each of the 4 LFOs has a BIPOLAR button on its page. On (default): the LFO swings both directions from center, e.g. modulating a dial clockwise and anticlockwise. Off: the same LFO shape is remapped to only ever add in one direction — useful for things like wavetable position, where you may want a one-way forward sweep rather than back-and-forth motion.
Macros — 4 knobs, each usable as a matrix source; route one macro to another macro's amount for chained control.
FX chain — reorder with ↑/↓; each effect has its own per-layer Send toggles (WT/FM/SMP) so it only affects the layers you choose.
MPC Pads — a 16-pad grid for one-shot sample triggering, with a LOCK to prevent accidental changes while performing.
Scope — shows a real-time oscilloscope of the master output as a thin white line, in a moveable floating window. Tap to open/close. Use the RADIAL button to switch to a radial (circular) scope instead, where the waveform wraps around a circle; deselect it to return to the linear display.
Hide Keyboard — next to PLAY. Collapses the on-screen keyboard and wheels to free up screen space; press again (it relabels itself Show Keyboard) to bring them back.
Presets — save, rename inline, and export the whole bank as a .zip (includes samples and imported wavetables) for backup or sharing.
